# Project profile template

Use this structure for each activity in Sections 5 and 6. Remove fields that genuinely do not apply; do not silently omit a risk field.

## [Project name]

**Color and scope:** [Green or Yellow; define the exact task covered]

**Reader payoff:** [What improves, and why this may be worth doing]

**Boundary:** [What nearby or similar work is outside this profile; when the category changes]

**Before starting:** [HDB permit/conditions check, licensed-person check, ownership permission, skill prerequisites, product instructions, work area, shutoff/isolation, and backup where applicable]

**Tools and materials:** [Only those necessary for the defined scope; distinguish required safety/test equipment]

**Time and cost:** [Range, locale, date, scope, assumptions, and source; leave blank until verified]

**Main hazards and controls:** [Plain-language hazards and how the scope avoids them; subject to qualified review]

**Method:** [A short, reviewable sequence for green/appropriate yellow work; no execution method for red work]

**Quality check:** [What the reader can actually observe or test, when to recheck, and what uncertainty remains]

**Stop and call a professional if:** [Specific signs, discoveries, or failed checks]

**Common mistakes:** [Likely error, consequence, prevention]

**Sources and review:** [Source IDs, jurisdiction, date checked, technical reviewer, review status]

## Definition of a finished profile

The profile describes a bounded job, has a category justified by the shared rule, states local-rule uncertainty, includes a meaningful stop condition, and has evidence for changeable or consequential claims. A reader can tell whether the job still fits the profile after opening the work area.
