# 4. General Safety, Legal, and Financial Foundations

Before doing a small job, learn enough about the flat to avoid turning it into a large one. Find the water stopcock, identify the consumer unit without opening it, know which services run near the work area, and keep the numbers for building management, the Town Council, and relevant licensed trades. Record the location of each shutoff in Appendix D. If you do not know what a valve or switch controls, do not rely on it as your only safety measure.

## 4.1 Permission and Responsibility

For an HDB flat, ask three separate questions: **Is the work allowed? Does it need HDB approval? Must a licensed person do it?** HDB says some renovation work needs a permit and work without one must still meet its conditions. HDB's permit guidance also identifies work for which the owner must engage a registered renovation contractor or a worker with the relevant licence.[^hdb-permit] Check the current HDB page for the exact task, flat type, and location; a table for a different work type is not enough.

Electrical and plumbing illustrate why these questions are separate. EMA directs consumers to Licensed Electrical Workers for electrical installation work, including additions and replacements of points.[^ema-electrical] PUB identifies listed simple plumbing works separately from regulated water-service and sanitary work.[^pub-plumbing] Do not infer permission to do an installation simply because HDB does not require a permit for that item. Keep permits, contractor details, invoices, product instructions, test records, and before-and-after photographs together.

HDB's bathroom rules deserve particular care. Its renovation information describes a three-year restriction on removing toilet wall and floor finishes in newly completed flats, intended to protect the waterproofing system. Work on bathroom finishes can require a permit and water test.[^hdb-bathroom] A bead of surface sealant is not the waterproofing membrane. If water appears below the floor, behind a wall, or in a neighbour's ceiling, stop cosmetic repair and arrange assessment.

## 4.2 Safe Preparation and Stop Signs

Choose a work area you can control. Clear trip hazards, protect children and pets, ventilate as the product label requires, and wear eye, hand, hearing, or respiratory protection appropriate to the task. Check the condition of tools and use a stable work platform. Do not drill until you have considered concealed wiring and pipes; HDB warns that pipe layouts may only be a guide and provides separate information for concealed services.[^hdb-plumbing]

For any task involving water, know how to stop the supply *before* loosening a fitting. HDB's home-care guidance says the stopcock at the PUB water sub-meter can cut supply when there is a leak.[^hdb-pipes] For electrical installation faults, damaged sockets, repeated trips, heat, or a burning smell, leave the installation work to a Licensed Electrical Worker. HDB's electrical maintenance guidance says only a licensed electrician can replace socket outlets.[^hdb-electrical-care] A gas smell or suspected gas leak is an emergency, not a project: avoid operating switches, move to a safe distance, and use the current gas-emergency guidance.[^ema-gas]

Your stop rule should be written before work begins. Stop if the observed condition differs from the plan, if the isolation method fails, if water cannot be shut off, if a hidden service is found, if a fitting is corroded or unstable, or if the result cannot be checked reliably. Secure the area and ask for help. The ability to stop safely is one of the conditions for a yellow task.

## 4.3 The Real Cost of DIY

Compare *complete* costs: materials, consumables, tool purchase or rental, disposal, travel, time, and a reasonable allowance for rework. Then compare the plausible cost of a mistake. Painting may offer a strong return because a poor finish is obvious and usually fixable. A leak inside a concealed space may be inexpensive to start and expensive to discover. The relevant question is not “Can I save the labour charge?” but “What outcome am I taking responsibility for?”

Use quotes to understand what a professional will actually deliver: scope, protection of the flat, testing, approvals, waste handling, timing, and warranty terms. Keep a record of what was agreed and what changed. A lower price is not comparable if it omits testing or leaves you with the risk of a hidden defect. Claims about insurance coverage or warranty loss must be checked against the actual policy and product terms; this book will not assume an automatic denial.
