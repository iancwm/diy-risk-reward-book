# 1. Executive Summary

The useful question for a new homeowner is not “Can I learn to do this?” It is “Should I do this particular job in this particular flat?” Painting a sound bedroom wall and altering a bathroom floor may both be called home improvement, but they have different failure paths. This book helps you separate the jobs you can learn with ordinary care from the jobs you should plan, brief, and commission.

The primary setting is an owner-occupied Singapore HDB flat. Its examples use metric units, Singapore dollars, and HDB conditions. A reader in another housing type can use the decision questions, but must check the rules for that property. Each colour in the book belongs to a **defined scope**, not to a trade name or a person's confidence. When the scope changes, the decision changes.

**Green** is a small, visible, reversible task that a prepared beginner is permitted to undertake and can check after completion. Painting a dry interior wall, changing cabinet hardware, and assembling freestanding furniture are possible examples. **Yellow** has a more consequential failure path but may be suitable if the exact work is allowed, the site conditions are known, and the homeowner has a safe way to isolate, stop, and verify it. An accessible like-for-like tap replacement may fit; a corroded shutoff or altered pipe does not. **Red** means the homeowner learns enough to specify and check the outcome while an appropriately qualified person executes the work. Electrical installation, piped gas, structural alteration, and failed bathroom waterproofing are red examples for homeowner execution.

The colours do not replace a rules check. Ask separately whether HDB approval is required, which HDB conditions apply, and who is authorised to carry out the work. HDB says work that does not need its renovation permit still has to meet its conditions.[^hdb-permit] Singapore's Energy Market Authority directs consumers to Licensed Electrical Workers for electrical installation work; a “no HDB permit” entry does not make that work a homeowner task.[^ema-electrical] PUB distinguishes listed simple plumbing works from regulated water-service and sanitary work.[^pub-plumbing] If you cannot establish the rule for the exact scope, pause and ask the authority or a qualified person.

Before buying materials, write one sentence describing the smallest useful result. Note what is included and excluded. Then ask: What could fail? Would I see the failure soon? Can I reverse it without opening a wall, floor, or system? Could someone else be affected? Do I have the skill, access, shutoff, and test needed for this site? A single serious or hidden failure path can end the DIY decision even when the work looks quick. A permit is a separate approval question; it is neither a safety rating nor a licence.

The book is arranged to support that decision. Sections 2–4 establish the scope, colour rule, and safety foundations. Sections 5–7 apply the rule to green, yellow, and red project families. Sections 8–9 provide the pre-project check and a gradual skill path. Sections 10–13 cover tools, illustrative decisions, real costs, and obligations to neighbours and future occupants. The appendices supply a master table, working forms, a glossary, references, and sample plans. Product prices in the examples are dated retail inputs, not promises about a finished job.

A good result may be a completed small repair. It may also be a decision to stop after finding a leak, a more precise brief for a plumber, or a safer room layout that avoids moving an electrical point. The homeowner still does important work: defining the outcome, checking authority, comparing complete scopes, watching for changes, and retaining records. **Own the brief. Hire the risk.**
