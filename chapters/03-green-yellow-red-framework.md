# 3. The Green / Yellow / Red Framework

The colour system is a way to choose your role in a project. It is not a badge of skill, a guarantee of safety, or permission to perform regulated work. Start by writing one sentence that defines the job: “Paint the dry bedroom wall” is more useful than “renovate the bedroom.” Then identify what that sentence might conceal. Will you open a wall, connect a pipe, disturb a waterproof layer, work above shoulder height, or affect a neighbour's flat? The answer can change the colour.

## 3.1 Green Light — Learn and Do Within a Narrow Scope

A green task is visible, accessible, allowed, and easy to check. A beginner can correct an ordinary mistake without opening a concealed system or exposing anyone to serious harm. Painting a sound interior surface, replacing a cabinet knob, and assembling freestanding storage are potential green tasks. The colour applies only while the conditions hold: surface preparation that reveals mould or a persistent leak is no longer a paint job.

Green does not mean careless. A ladder, drill, blade, chemical product, or heavy item can turn a simple task into an injury. Read the product instructions, protect the room, use suitable equipment, and stop when the actual job differs from the one you planned. HDB's building-work guidance includes conditions for fixtures and fixings, including special considerations for the household shelter; a “no permit” table entry is not a blanket approval for every drilling location.[^hdb-building]

## 3.2 Yellow Light — Do Only If the Conditions Are Met

A yellow task has a meaningful failure path, but its risk may be controlled by a narrow scope, preparation, and a reliable check. A listed simple plumbing replacement, a moderate wall mounting, or a non-wet-area tile finish could be yellow for an HDB beginner after checking the exact rules and site. The profile must state the required skill, isolation method, test, and stop signs. If any condition is missing, the homeowner's role becomes planning and hiring.

Yellow is not a halfway point on the way to doing everything yourself. Some yellow-looking jobs in a generic DIY guide are professional work in Singapore. For example, EMA tells consumers to use a Licensed Electrical Worker for installation work such as replacing a switch, socket outlet, or lighting point.[^ema-electrical] This book therefore treats such jobs as homeowner briefing and professional execution, regardless of how simple a video makes them appear.

## 3.3 Red Light — Own the Brief, Commission the Work

Red describes a scope where the failure may be severe, hidden, hard to reverse, or subject to licensed or specialised execution. The homeowner can learn the purpose of the work, the likely hazards, the questions to ask, and the documents to keep. The homeowner does not learn a DIY execution method from this book.

Electrical installation, gas service work, structural alteration, and a failed bathroom waterproofing system are red examples in the HDB setting. PUB separates listed simple plumbing works from regulated water-service and sanitary works that require a Licensed Plumber; “plumbing” is therefore too broad a label to colour as one project.[^pub-plumbing] HDB also sets separate permit and technical conditions for various building, electrical, plumbing, and gas works.[^hdb-permit]

Red is not a judgment about a reader's intelligence. It recognises that a homeowner may never see a loose concealed connection, a membrane defect, or a gradual leak until the consequences are expensive. A professional's licence, testing, and responsibility are part of the work being purchased.

## 3.4 A Decision Rule You Can Use

Use the following gates in order. Do not add low scores to cancel a serious stop factor.

1. **Describe the smallest useful scope.** What will change, and what will remain untouched? If the job requires an unknown addition, classify the expanded scope again.
2. **Check authority.** Is the work allowed in this flat? Does HDB require a permit or impose a condition? Does the task require a licensed worker? Are there product or warranty conditions? If you cannot answer, pause and ask the appropriate authority or professional.
3. **Screen for severe or hidden outcomes.** Could a mistake cause electric shock, fire, gas escape, a fall, structural failure, concealed water damage, or harm to another flat? If so, treat it as red unless a qualified assessment establishes a genuinely separate, safe homeowner task.
4. **Judge ordinary failure.** How likely is a mistake for a beginner? Would you notice it before it causes damage? Can you restore the original condition without destructive work? How costly is the plausible worst case, not merely the average mistake?
5. **Check capability and verification.** Do you have the needed knowledge, time, tools, safe access, and a test you understand? Can you stop without leaving the home unsafe or without water/power? If not, hire.
6. **Record the decision.** Use the pre-project form in Appendix B. Write the exact stop signs and who you will call if they appear.

| Factor | Green tends to look like | Yellow tends to look like | Red signal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Consequence | Cosmetic or local and cheap to correct | Meaningful property damage or minor injury possible | Serious injury, fire, gas, structural, or multi-flat damage possible |
| Detectability | Result is fully visible | Result needs a defined test or recheck | Failure can remain concealed or emerge late |
| Reversibility | Original condition is easy to restore | Repair is possible but costly | Repair requires opening finishes or systems |
| Authority | Homeowner task within applicable conditions | Conditions or approval must be checked | Licensed/specialist execution, prohibited scope, or unclear authority |
| Skill and tools | Ordinary tools and simple checks | Specific practice and test equipment | Specialised competence, inspection, or certification |

The table is a prompt, not a numeric score. One red signal is enough to stop and seek a qualified opinion. Where a project mixes colours, divide the work honestly. You might select a fixture, document the existing condition, and compare quotes while a Licensed Electrical Worker makes the electrical connection. That is still an active, informed homeowner role.
