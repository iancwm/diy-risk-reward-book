# 11. Case Studies

The six cases below are **illustrative HDB-flat scenarios**, not reports of actual households or verified savings. They show how the same decision rule changes the homeowner's role when the scope changes. Any price in a case is a dated retail example or an explicitly hypothetical input, not a market quote for installation.

## 11.1 A TV Mount That Became a Picture-Hanging Job

**Brief.** A new homeowner wants a television above a desk and three framed pictures on the nearby wall. The TV bracket would put a heavy, projecting load on an unfamiliar partition, close to a possible concealed cable route. The pictures are light, and their proposed positions can be checked against the wall and HDB fixing conditions.[^hdb-building]

**Decision.** The homeowner splits the request. The bounded picture hanging is green once wall and fixings are confirmed. The television mount is yellow or professional work until the substrate, load, service route, and bracket are verified. There is no prize for treating both as one “mounting” job.

**Planning estimate.** Allow about an hour for measuring and the three light pictures, plus S$___ for suitable fixings. Request a separate TV-mount quote covering wall assessment, bracket compatibility, installation, and testing; do not treat the picture-fixing cost as its estimate.

**Illustrative result.** The pictures go up after a small test fixing. A contractor checks the TV location and proposes a different wall with a clear mounting scope. The homeowner keeps the bracket instructions and installation details. **Lesson:** the object, load, and wall determine the colour; a successful light fixing says little about a TV.

## 11.2 Recaulking a Visible Bathroom Edge

**Brief.** An old bead of sealant has lifted at the edge of a shower screen. The tiles are firm, the adjacent wall is dry, and there is no reported leak below. The homeowner wants a cleaner joint, not a bathroom renovation.

**Decision.** Removing and replacing a short *visible* seal may fit a bounded green or careful yellow task after the product instructions and cure time are checked. The homeowner records the starting condition and watches for recurring moisture. HDB's bathroom waterproofing system lies beneath the finishes; a surface bead does not repair it.[^hdb-bathroom]

**Planning estimate.** The example silicone tube in Section 12 is S$6.50, before removal tools, preparation, and time. Allow one short work session plus the maker's full cure period; set S$___ aside for materials and reassess if moisture returns.

**Illustrative result.** The bead looks neat, but a damp mark reappears after use. The homeowner stops repeating the caulk job and arranges an assessment of the water path. **Lesson:** a good finish is not proof that a concealed leak has been solved. The changed evidence changes the category.

## 11.3 Replacing a Kitchen Tap

**Brief.** A kitchen mixer is worn, and the cabinet below is dry. The stopcock works and the connections are visible. PUB lists tap replacement among simple plumbing works, while regulated pipe changes require a Licensed Plumber.[^pub-plumbing]

**Decision.** The homeowner treats a like-for-like tap as yellow: confirm exact scope, choose a compatible fitting, protect the cabinet, and plan repeat leak checks. A current retailer listing shows one basic kitchen tap at S$59; that is a product example, not the complete project cost.[^retail-tap]

**Planning estimate.** Product S$59; compatible parts S$___; plumber assessment and replacement S$___; total S$___ after a quote. Reserve a half day for access, checks, and leak observation, with the actual visit time confirmed by the plumber.

**Illustrative result.** During the initial check, the shutoff valve appears corroded. The homeowner stops before disconnecting anything and obtains a plumber's assessment of the valve and tap together. **Lesson:** the cheapest safe decision may be to stop early. The S$59 product price did not price the condition of the existing plumbing.

## 11.4 A Light Switch That Needed a Licensed Worker

**Brief.** A switch feels loose and sometimes fails to operate a light. A foreign DIY video makes replacement look like a short task. EMA includes switch replacement in electrical installation work for a Licensed Electrical Worker.[^ema-electrical]

**Decision.** The homeowner leaves the switch unused, photographs the faceplate without opening it, and briefs a Licensed Electrical Worker. The request includes the symptom, the flat's location, the desired replacement, and a question about testing and any HDB condition.

**Planning estimate.** Allow time for a worker visit and request a quote that separates diagnosis, parts, installation, and testing: S$___ total. The homeowner's own preparation is a short symptom record, not repair time.

**Illustrative result.** The worker finds that the visible switch is not the only issue and proposes the correct repair. The homeowner keeps the invoice and test information. **Lesson:** a component's visible size does not describe the whole failure path. The homeowner saved time by giving the worker a precise brief.

## 11.5 Moving an Outlet for a Desk

**Brief.** A desk would fit better if a socket were moved half a metre. The homeowner initially calls this a “small adjustment.” It is actually a change to fixed electrical installation, potentially including wiring route, load, wall finish, and HDB conditions.[^ema-electrical][^hdb-electrical]

**Decision.** Red for homeowner execution. The homeowner first tests a furniture layout that uses the existing socket safely, without overloaded adaptors or a cord across a walkway. If that does not work, a Licensed Electrical Worker quotes an approved new layout and explains the required testing.

**Planning estimate.** Try a 30-minute layout check at no material cost. If it fails, obtain a written S$___ quote for the electrical scope, wall making-good, approvals if required, and testing.

**Illustrative result.** Rearranging the desk solves the problem without electrical work. **Lesson:** define the desired outcome before committing to a risky method. “Make the desk usable” was the true brief; “move the outlet” was only one proposed solution.

## 11.6 A Shower Leak Affecting the Flat Below

**Brief.** The lower-floor neighbour reports a damp bathroom ceiling. The upper-floor homeowner sees no obvious water on the surface and considers painting or resealing the shower edge. HDB explains that ceiling leaks often involve the waterproofing membrane or screed and may require a joint investigation and repair.[^hdb-leaks]

**Decision.** Red for DIY execution. The homeowner records dates and photographs, speaks with the neighbour, and arranges a contractor assessment of the source. The scope includes how the leak will be confirmed, the proposed repair, relevant HDB conditions, and the final water test where required.[^hdb-bathroom]

**Planning estimate.** Record and report the symptoms promptly. Seek a written assessment S$___ and repair S$___ only after the source and responsibility are established; a tube of sealant is not a credible budget for this scope.

**Illustrative result.** The source is traced to the floor system rather than the visible edge. A surface caulk job would have delayed the correct repair. **Lesson:** hidden water can affect another household; the evidence, testing, and shared plan are as important as the new finish.

These scenarios do not prove a universal saving from DIY or from hiring. They show a repeatable habit: define the result, test the scope against rules and risk, and change course when new evidence appears.
