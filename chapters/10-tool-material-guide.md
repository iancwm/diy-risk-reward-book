# 10. Tool and Material Guide

Buy tools for the projects you will actually repeat, not for a future version of yourself who can do every trade. A first-time HDB homeowner can maintain a useful kit without owning electrical test equipment, pipe tools, or demolition equipment. The project profile should determine each purchase. If a job needs a tool you do not know how to use safely, that is a reason to reconsider the job, not merely a shopping prompt.

## A small starter kit

Begin with a tape measure, pencil, level, screwdrivers, adjustable spanner, pliers, utility knife with a retractable blade, a stable step stool, protective sheets, and basic eye and hand protection. Add a drill/driver only when you have a confirmed wall material, an allowed fixing location, and a project that needs it. HDB's building-work guidance includes special rules for the household shelter and other fixtures; HDB also warns that concealed pipe plans are only guides.[^hdb-building][^hdb-plumbing] A stud finder or cable detector can add information but cannot prove a wall is free of services.

Choose the correct tool size rather than more force. A well-fitting screwdriver is less likely to strip a screw. A level and a second measurement prevent more rework than a larger drill. Keep blades sharp and guarded, replace damaged leads, and store small parts where children cannot reach them. For wet-area cleaning or drain debris, use suitable gloves and eye protection, and keep chemical products in their labelled containers.

## Add tools by project family

For painting, buy or borrow a tray, roller, brush, scraper for sound surface preparation, sanding block, and drop cloth. Match primer and filler to the substrate and coating instructions. For decorative caulking, use a caulk gun, suitable removal tool, cleaning materials, and backing material only where the sealant maker calls for it. Do not use a surface sealant as a repair for the concealed bathroom waterproofing system.

For simple plumbing care, a basin or tray, cloth, correct spanner, and a torch may be enough. The critical control is a working water shutoff and a later leak check, not a large wrench. PUB's list separates simple works from regulated water-service and sanitary plumbing.[^pub-plumbing] A pipe wrench, compression tool, or torch does not turn a regulated or concealed job into a homeowner task. Likewise, a voltage tester or multimeter does not authorise electrical installation; EMA directs consumers to Licensed Electrical Workers for such work.[^ema-electrical]

For mounting, buy fixings *after* confirming the wall and the load. A packet labelled “universal” is not proof of suitability. Check the shelf or bracket maker's instructions, including whether wall screws are supplied. Work at height, overhead loads, household-shelter surfaces, and uncertain concealed routes require a separate risk decision.

## Choose materials by function

Read the whole label or technical sheet: compatible surfaces, preparation, application conditions, cure or drying time, cleaning, and storage. Similar-looking tubes can have different uses. A paintable interior gap filler is not interchangeable with sanitary silicone; neither repairs a failed wet-area membrane. Tile adhesive and grout must fit the tile, substrate, and location. Flexible plumbing hoses must match the connection and intended service; do not solve a mismatch with an improvised adaptor.

Buy enough to finish a small job, with a modest allowance for cutting and mistakes. Keep the batch or colour reference for later touch-up. Before buying a finish, inspect the underlying surface. A wet wall or loose tile is a diagnosis problem, not an invitation to buy a stronger coating.

## Buy, borrow, or hire?

Buy a durable tool when you expect repeat use, can store it safely, and know the work is within your scope. Borrow or rent for a rare, bounded task where you already have the skill. Hire the *service* when the outcome needs licensed execution, specialist inspection, or testing. The purchase price of a tool is only one part of the comparison: include consumables, storage, learning time, and the cost of using it incorrectly.
