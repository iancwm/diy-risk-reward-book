# 7. Red Light Activities — Why They Are Not Worth Learning for DIY Execution

Red is the point at which the homeowner's useful role changes. You still decide what outcome you want, notice symptoms, ask questions, compare scope, and keep records. You do not turn a serious, hidden, or professionally controlled task into a first personal attempt. The reason is not that a competent person could never learn a trade. It is that one HDB project seldom gives a beginner enough practice, testing capacity, or authority to justify the possible harm to occupants, neighbours, and future owners.

## 7.1 What Makes a Task Red?

A task is red for homeowner execution when a plausible mistake could cause severe injury, fire, gas escape, structural damage, or water damage that remains concealed until it reaches another flat. A licensing or specialist requirement is also a red boundary for the unqualified homeowner. The colour applies to the **work being performed**. You may have a green planning task within a red project: photographing a leak is different from opening the wet floor to repair its membrane.

A permit is a separate decision. Some HDB work needs a permit and can proceed when properly approved and performed by the right people. Some work has no HDB permit requirement yet still needs a licensed worker or must meet technical conditions. HDB says renovation items without a permit remain subject to its guidelines.[^hdb-permit] Never use “no permit” as a synonym for “safe for me to do.”

## 7.2 The HDB Red Boundaries

**Electrical installation and the consumer unit.** Replacing or adding switches, socket outlets, lighting points, wiring, circuit breakers, or work in the consumer unit belongs with a Licensed Electrical Worker. EMA lists these as electrical work for licensed workers; HDB adds its own approval and testing conditions where applicable.[^ema-electrical][^hdb-electrical] The homeowner can describe the fault, choose a fitting, verify the worker's licence, ask what testing and records are included, and leave unsafe fittings unused. A burning smell, heat, damage, or repeated breaker trip is a prompt for assessment, not a trial repair.

**Piped gas and gas appliances.** EMA says piped-gas appliances require installation by a licensed worker, who provides a written statement that the appliance has been tested and is safe to use.[^ema-gas-worker] The homeowner can record the model, location, symptoms, and supplier, then commission the right worker. A suspected leak is an emergency: follow EMA's current gas-safety instructions and call the emergency line from a safe distance.[^ema-gas-safety] Do not learn gas work by experimenting on a live installation.

**Structural and load-bearing work.** Do not cut, hack, or alter a reinforced-concrete slab, beam, column, wall, or uncertain support. HDB requires prior approval for demolition or hacking of walls and may require a Qualified Person for a proposal; structural members are not a homeowner trial area.[^hdb-building][^hdb-permit] A visible crack, spalling concrete, or an apparently “non-load-bearing” element needs its own assessment. The homeowner's useful job is to document the location and change over time, keep people away from loose material, and obtain the correct HDB/qualified-person direction.

**Bathroom waterproofing and cross-flat leaks.** Cosmetic surface sealant is not the membrane below bathroom finishes. HDB's rules describe a waterproofing system, restrictions on replacing new-flat bathroom finishes, and a water test after replacement.[^hdb-bathroom] HDB also explains that ceiling leaks often involve the waterproof membrane or screed and may require cooperation between flats or the Town Council, depending on the source.[^hdb-leaks] If water appears in a lower flat, under a finish, or repeatedly around a floor trap, the job is diagnosis and coordinated repair, not a thicker bead of caulk.

**Regulated plumbing and concealed drainage.** PUB lists some simple plumbing works separately, but regulated water-service and sanitary works require a Licensed Plumber.[^pub-plumbing] Moving a pipe, changing a toilet bowl, opening a concealed line, or altering drainage in a wall or slab is outside the simple accessory profiles. The homeowner can prepare photographs, note the leak or blockage pattern, and ask the plumber how the installation will be tested and documented.

**Exterior windows, air-conditioners, and common property.** A loose HDB window can fall onto people below. HDB directs owners to approved window contractors for repair or replacement and requires trained installers for exterior air-conditioning units.[^hdb-windows][^hdb-aircon] Do not lean out to seal a frame, adjust a failing window, or handle an exterior condenser. For a leak that may come from the roof, facade, or another common-property source, record it and contact the responsible party, including the Town Council where appropriate.[^hdb-leaks]

**Suspected asbestos and other hazardous conditions.** Do not disturb material you suspect may contain asbestos. MOM says an asbestos surveyor can confirm the material and an Approved Asbestos-Removal Contractor is required if confirmed material is to be removed.[^mom-asbestos] For other unknown coatings, substantial mould, or contaminated material, the first task is to identify the source and appropriate specialist, not to sand, scrape, or seal over it. The book should not claim every mould or lead-paint situation has the same Singapore licensing rule; the exact material and extent need assessment.

**Refrigerant and complex ventilation.** Cleaning an accessible air-conditioner filter is a different task from opening a refrigerant system, changing an exterior unit, or redesigning ventilation. HDB says external air-conditioner installation requires a trained installer and stresses inspection of brackets and leaks.[^hdb-aircon] The homeowner can report symptoms and compare service scope, but should not use appliance maintenance as a route into sealed-system repair.

## 7.3 Why the DIY Saving Is Misleading

The arithmetic is asymmetric. A green mistake tends to be visible: the colour is wrong or a handle sits crooked. A red mistake can look successful on the day and fail later inside a wall, below tiles, or at a connection. The homeowner may not have the tools or access to verify it. Another flat can suffer damage before anyone finds the source.

The true comparison is between the complete professional price and the complete DIY exposure: materials, tools, time, testing, potential rework, and the credible cost of a hidden failure. It also includes the value of documented approval, trained execution, and a defined party to call if something goes wrong. Do not assume an insurance claim will automatically be denied, or that a contractor's warranty covers every consequence; read the actual policy and contract. The decision still favours professional execution where the downside is severe or hard to detect.

## 7.4 The “Just Enough to Be Dangerous” Trap

A video can show the visible sequence but not the condition of your flat's wiring, pipe routes, wall construction, waterproofing, or prior alterations. It may be filmed under another country's rules. A successful-looking finish is not a test of a concealed connection. If a guide teaches the steps but cannot tell you how to confirm the site is suitable, who is authorised to do the work, and how the result is certified, it is not enough for a red project.

Use online material to learn vocabulary and formulate questions, then verify the answer against current HDB and regulator guidance and the professional's site assessment. Confidence gained from watching should not be mistaken for evidence that the job is safe.

## 7.5 Learn Enough to Commission Well

The homeowner can bring a one-page brief to the first discussion:

1. **Describe the outcome and symptoms.** What is wrong, when did it start, and who else is affected? Include dated photographs without opening a hazardous system.
2. **Ask who will perform and approve the work.** Verify the relevant licence, HDB contractor listing, permit, or Qualified Person where the scope calls for one.
3. **Ask how the result will be checked.** Request the proposed test, inspection, handover document, and what happens if it fails.
4. **Compare complete scopes.** Confirm protection of the flat, access to concealed areas, disposal, reinstatement, timing, and responsibility for damage.
5. **Keep the record.** Save the approval, quotation, variations, photographs, test evidence, invoice, and warranty terms.

Trade school or supervised apprenticeship can be valuable for a person pursuing that trade. It does not convert an unlicensed homeowner's HDB project into an authorised training site. For this book's reader, the strongest result is often a clear brief and a competent professional. Section 8 turns that judgement into forms you can complete before commissioning work.
