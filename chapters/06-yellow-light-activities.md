# 6. Yellow Light Activities — Learn Carefully

Yellow profiles require a decision before the tool comes out. A job may be yellow because water, weight, a hidden surface, or a difficult finish makes a mistake more costly. Some items from the original outline are **not DIY execution projects in Singapore**; the yellow skill is then planning, hiring, and verifying professional work. The time bands below are editorial allowances for a single bounded job, excluding shopping, approvals, cure time, and discoveries. Singapore-dollar ranges await a sourced local price check. Use Appendix B for each project.

## 6.1 Tap Replacement; Toilet and Garbage-Disposal Exceptions

**Homeowner scope.** PUB lists replacement or removal of a tap or mixer among simple plumbing works, but the job is yellow for a beginner because water must be isolated and every disturbed joint checked.[^pub-plumbing] Start with an accessible, like-for-like tap and a sound shutoff. Confirm that the chosen model fits the existing mounting and connections and that HDB's plumbing conditions apply to the planned scope.[^hdb-plumbing]

**Preparation and result.** Photograph the existing arrangement, identify the stopcock, protect the cabinet, and read the tap maker's instructions. If the shutoff works, remove only the components included in the simple replacement, keep seals in their specified positions, and reassemble without forcing threads. Restore water gradually, observe every joint while the tap runs, then check again after ordinary use. A dry cabinet after one minute is not enough evidence of a slow leak.

**Stop and hire.** Stop if a valve will not close, a pipe moves, a connection is corroded or non-matching, the leak is concealed, or the job expands into pipe alteration. Replacing a toilet bowl is not on PUB's listed simple-work examples and should be scoped with a Licensed Plumber rather than treated as the same task. A garbage disposal is uncommon in HDB flats; any drainage or electrical changes make it a professional project. **Planning allowance:** half a day for a straightforward accessible tap, plus later leak checks. **Common mistake:** buying a fitting before measuring the existing connections.

## 6.2 Electrical Replacements — Professional Execution

**Homeowner scope.** A switch, socket outlet, dimmer, or lighting-point replacement can look like a simple exchange, but EMA directs consumers to engage a Licensed Electrical Worker for electrical installation work, including such replacements.[^ema-electrical] HDB's electrical page adds a separate permit-and-condition check.[^hdb-electrical] The homeowner's yellow task is to define the outcome, engage the correct worker, and retain the completion information. The physical replacement is red for DIY execution.

**Preparation and result.** Record the existing fitting's location, symptoms, and photographs without opening it. Tell the worker whether a device is loose, hot, discoloured, intermittently working, or repeatedly tripping a breaker. Ask for the licence details, the exact scope, whether HDB approval or other testing is needed, and what will be supplied. Keep the quotation and any test or completion record. After the worker has finished, check the user-visible function and report any heat, smell, flicker, or repeated trip.

**Stop and hire.** Do not diagnose by removing a faceplate or touching wiring. An unsafe fitting should be left unused until assessed. **Planning allowance:** time to brief, obtain a quote, arrange access, and inspect the finished user-facing result; the worker estimates execution time. **Common mistake:** equating “HDB permit not required” with “homeowner installation allowed.”

## 6.3 Drywall or Gypsum-Board Patch

**Homeowner scope.** A small hole in a dry, non-structural gypsum-board partition may be a reasonable yellow practice project. Many HDB walls are not drywall; confirm the material before choosing this method. A damp patch, a crack at a structural element, a hole with visible wiring or pipework, or loose concrete is not a cosmetic drywall repair. HDB distinguishes hairline wall cracks from wider ones that need contractor attention.[^hdb-walls]

**Preparation and result.** Photograph the defect and establish that it is dry and stable. Use a patch and compound suited to the board and the maker's instructions. Keep the repair within the surface layer; do not enlarge a hole simply to make a convenient shape if you do not know what lies behind it. Apply thin coats, allow each to dry, sand with suitable dust control, prime, and paint. Judge the result in side light; a smooth patch is a finish outcome, not evidence that a recurring crack has been solved.

**Stop and hire.** Stop for moisture, repeated movement, bulging, loose concrete, or any concealed service. **Planning allowance:** several short sessions across one to two days because drying time governs the job. **Common mistake:** applying one thick coat and sanding before it is dry.

## 6.4 Kitchen Backsplash Tile Finish

**Homeowner scope.** A small backsplash on a dry kitchen wall may be yellow if the existing surface is sound, the selected product is suitable, and the work fits HDB's current wall-finish conditions. HDB distinguishes laying finishes over existing finishes from replacing internal wall finishes; the latter may require a permit.[^hdb-building] This profile does not include a shower, bathroom, wet-room waterproofing, wall hacking, or moving an electrical point.

**Preparation and result.** Draw the layout to scale, locate edges and openings, and check that existing cabinets and fittings remain accessible. Confirm the permitted wall build-up and whether the planned work is an overlay or replacement. Obtain the tile and adhesive maker's substrate, joint, and cure instructions. Try a dry layout before mixing any product. Keep cuts and dust controlled; arrange a qualified worker for any electrical fitting that must be altered. After installation, inspect adhesion, clean joints, and allow full cure before heavy cleaning or loading the area.

**Stop and hire.** Stop for a loose or damp substrate, unexpected layers, an exposed service, or a design that depends on removing existing finishes without the appropriate HDB decision. **Planning allowance:** two or more sessions plus curing for a small splash zone. **Common mistake:** buying tile before calculating edge pieces and the finished thickness around fittings.

## 6.5 Smart Controls and Doorbells — Thermostat Exception

**Homeowner scope.** A battery doorbell or plug-in control can be green when installed without fixed wiring. The yellow questions are whether it must be fixed to an HDB surface, whether the signal and privacy settings work, and whether the desired location affects a neighbour or common area. A hardwired doorbell, transformer, or other fixed electrical alteration is for a Licensed Electrical Worker.[^ema-electrical]

**Preparation and result.** Check the product's power method, mounting instructions, network requirements, and data settings. Test the device indoors before making a hole. If it needs a fixing, use the mounting decision in Section 5.3; if it records a shared corridor, consider the people and rules affected before choosing the view. Confirm that the device works after a network interruption and that the household knows how to operate it without the app.

**Exception.** Smart thermostats are uncommon in many HDB flats because the cooling system may not use the low-voltage thermostat arrangement assumed by overseas DIY guides. Do not adapt a foreign wiring video to an air-conditioner control board; ask the equipment maker or an appropriately qualified installer. **Planning allowance:** one to three hours for a battery or plug-in setup, excluding network problems. **Common mistake:** buying a device before checking the existing power and mounting arrangement.

## 6.6 Exterior Caulking and Sealing — Professional Boundary

**Homeowner scope.** In an HDB flat, the exterior envelope, window perimeter, and facade are not a normal homeowner caulking project. Water may be entering through a window assembly, facade joint, or concealed route; an interior bead can hide the symptom without fixing the cause. HDB says window repair requires an approved window contractor and provides a specific process for window works.[^hdb-windows]

**Preparation and result.** Photograph when and where water appears, note whether it coincides with rain, and protect belongings. From a safe position inside the flat, check whether the wet area is near a window, ceiling, or pipe, without leaning out or removing a window component. Report the evidence to the responsible party and ask the contractor to identify the source, the approved repair scope, and how the result will be checked. Keep any report and warranty terms.

**Stop and hire.** Do not work outside a window, seal a facade joint, or cover ongoing dampness with interior paint. **Planning allowance:** one inspection and briefing session for the homeowner; repair timing is contractor-led. **Common mistake:** treating every rain-related mark as a gap that can be filled from inside.

## 6.7 Floating Shelves and Heavier Storage

**Homeowner scope.** A freestanding bookcase belongs in Section 5.8. A floating shelf transfers load into a wall, often through hidden brackets, so it is yellow even when the shelf looks light. Confirm the wall type, load including future contents, fixing design, and concealed-service risk. HDB's building guidelines set conditions for fixtures and special rules for household-shelter surfaces.[^hdb-building]

**Preparation and result.** Ask what the shelf will carry at its heaviest, not what it carries on installation day. Compare the bracket maker's instructions with the actual substrate; do not assume an anchor rating from a different wall material applies. Locate the fixing points away from uncertain pipes and wiring. If the wall, service route, or load is unclear, commission the installation. For a bounded homeowner installation, document the fixings used and test with a controlled load before ordinary use; recheck for movement after a week.

**Stop and hire.** Stop for a hollow or damaged substrate, a heavy television or overhead load, any household-shelter uncertainty, or a fixing that loosens. **Planning allowance:** two to four hours for one modest shelf after the wall is confirmed. **Common mistake:** ignoring leverage: the farther a load sits from the wall, the greater the demand on the fixings.

## 6.8 Visible Flexible-Hose Replacement

**Homeowner scope.** PUB lists replacement or removal of a flexible hose connected to a tap among simple plumbing works.[^pub-plumbing] A like-for-like visible hose between accessible fittings can be a yellow beginner task because a small leak may run unnoticed inside a cabinet. Replacing a stop valve, soldered or concealed line, or any pipe within a wall is outside this profile.

**Preparation and result.** Check the hose maker's size and application, photograph its route, and confirm the upstream shutoff works. Protect the cabinet and keep a tray or cloth ready for residual water. Replace only the listed accessible component using the specified seals and connection method; avoid twisting the hose or overtightening a fitting. Restore water gradually. Inspect dry tissue placed under each joint while the fitting is used, then check again later and the next day.

**Stop and hire.** Stop if the valve is seized, a fixed pipe turns with the nut, there is corrosion, the replacement requires an adaptor of uncertain suitability, or water appears from behind the cabinet. **Planning allowance:** one to two hours plus follow-up checks. **Common mistake:** confirming the joint only while the tap is off, when the leak may appear under flow or movement.

## 6.9 Appliance Installation and Connection

**Homeowner scope.** A freestanding appliance that uses an existing suitable socket and a maker-approved simple hose connection may be manageable after careful checks. PUB's simple-work list includes certain appliance connecting hoses; it does not make every appliance installation simple.[^pub-plumbing] In an HDB flat, gas appliances, fixed electrical wiring, new outlets, ducting changes, and exterior air-conditioner installation require the relevant professional scope.[^ema-electrical][^hdb-aircon]

**Preparation and result.** Before delivery, compare the model's dimensions, electrical rating, water/drain arrangement, ventilation clearances, and the maker's installation instructions with the actual site. Identify who is responsible for delivery, connection, leak testing, removal of the old unit, and disposal. If a connection is within the listed simple plumbing scope and you choose to do it, check water isolation and inspect it after operation. Keep the receipt, model number, installation record, and warranty terms together.

**Stop and hire.** Stop when a plug does not match, a socket heats or trips, a drain must be altered, the appliance is hardwired, or the installation needs a new gas, electrical, or air-conditioning connection. **Planning allowance:** a half-day for delivery, fit, and checks; actual installation time depends on the product. **Common mistake:** measuring the appliance body but not the access route, door swing, hoses, and service clearances.

## 6.10 Deck and Fence Staining — Brief Exception

Decks and fences are not normal HDB-flat features. Keep this original-outline topic as a transfer example: on a different property, surface staining can be a finish job, but structural rot, height, and shared boundaries change the decision. In an HDB flat, do not treat a facade, common corridor, window, or balcony barrier as a personal deck or fence. A safer analogous project is refinishing a small freestanding indoor timber item using the product's ventilation and fire precautions. Recheck the work's rules if you later apply this framework outside HDB housing.
