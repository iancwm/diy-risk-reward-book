# 7. Red Light Activities — Why They Are Not Worth Learning
## 7.1 Definition
- Work where failure can kill, maim, burn, flood, collapse, poison, or create legal liability.
- Work requiring license, permit, inspection, or specialized insurance.

## 7.2 Common Examples
- Electrical panel work, new circuits, mains wiring.
- Gas lines, gas appliances, gas leaks.
- Structural changes, load-bearing walls, foundations, stairs.
- Waterproofing showers, wet rooms, roofs, balconies.
- Moving plumbing/drainage inside walls or slabs.
- HVAC refrigerant, duct design, major ventilation.
- Asbestos, lead paint, mold remediation.
- Anything requiring a permit or inspection.

## 7.3 Why Not Worth It
- **Asymmetric risk:** small savings vs. catastrophic loss.
- **Life safety:** fire, shock, explosion, collapse, carbon monoxide.
- **Hidden failure:** slow leaks, overheating, mold, structural fatigue.
- **Legal:** fines, stop-work orders, unpermitted work, liability.
- **Insurance:** denied claims, canceled policies, personal liability.
- **Financial:** repair cost exceeds savings; resale and inspection problems.
- **Warranty:** voided manufacturer and contractor warranties.
- **Skill/tool/time:** specialized tools, certification, years of experience.
- **Opportunity cost:** pro does it faster, safer, and with warranty.
- **Ethical:** risk to family, neighbors, future owners.

## 7.4 The “Just Enough to Be Dangerous” Trap
- YouTube makes red work look easy.
- You cannot see code, load, pressure, or hidden conditions.
- Confidence without competence is the main cause of DIY disasters.

## 7.5 When Red Learning Is Acceptable
- General knowledge to hire and supervise better.
- Supervised apprenticeship or trade school.
- Owner-builder with permits and inspections.
- Never for unpermitted, uninspected DIY execution.
