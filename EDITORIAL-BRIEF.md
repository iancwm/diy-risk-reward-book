# Editorial brief — working draft

## Book promise

Help a residential reader decide which home projects to do, which to approach only under defined conditions, and which to commission from a qualified professional. The reader should finish with a sound decision and a useful project brief, even when they do not perform the work.

**Central line:** Own the brief. Hire the risk.

## Editorial scope

- Owner-occupied Singapore HDB flats: maintenance, repair, and improvement. Other housing types and rentals may appear only in a short scope note, not as primary examples.
- Teach decision-making, preparation, inspection, and communication with contractors.
- Green and some yellow profiles may give practical steps after appropriate safety review. Red sections explain hazards, project scoping, and hiring questions, not execution instructions.
- Commercial work, gas, service or panel electrical work, structural design, refrigerant systems, and regulated hazardous-material remediation stay outside DIY instruction.

## Provisional category rule

1. Identify the project and what is **actually** being altered, including concealed parts and nearby systems.
2. Check immediate stop factors: credible risk of death or serious injury; gas or combustion; structural support; service or panel electrical work; concealed water damage or waterproofing; regulated hazardous material; or a condition the homeowner cannot isolate, inspect, and test. Put such work in **red** and seek a qualified professional.
3. Check Singapore regulator and building rules, ownership/tenancy terms, manufacturer instructions, insurer requirements, tools, training, and a backup plan. A permit or inspection requirement prompts this check; it does not alone determine the color. If a licence, approval, or competence is missing, stop and engage an authorised person.
4. Where failure is visible, limited, reversible, and inexpensive, and the work is allowed, assign **green**. Where failure can cause meaningful damage or injury but safe limits and verification are available to a trained reader, assign **yellow** with explicit conditions.
5. If a project crosses categories, use the highest risk part for the overall decision, or split it into homeowner and professional scopes. The color belongs to a specific scope and context, not a project name forever.

This is an editorial classification for drafting, not a substitute for Singapore law or the rules of a particular building. It must be checked by relevant trades before publication.

### Singapore-specific classification decisions

- EMA says work on a home's electrical installation, including replacement of switches, socket outlets, and lighting points, should be carried out or supervised by a Licensed Electrical Worker. Section 6.2 should therefore become a homeowner **pro-commissioning** profile for the Singapore edition, not a DIY replacement guide. Plug-in devices and bulb changes remain separate scopes. [EMA consumer guidance](https://www.ema.gov.sg/consumer-information/electricity/engaging-licensed-workers)
- PUB distinguishes regulated plumbing from listed simple plumbing works that handymen can do. A tap or showerhead swap may fit a bounded reader project; toilet-bowl and pipe work require a scope-by-scope check and may need a Licensed Plumber. Reclassify Section 6.1 and Section 5.5 at task level. [PUB plumbing works](https://www.pub.gov.sg/Professionals/Requirements/Licensed-Plumbers/Plumbing-Works)
- HDB says some work needs prior approval and some work without a permit still has conditions. Each HDB project profile needs a separate “permit/conditions/licensed person” check. [HDB renovation permit guidance](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/managing-my-home/renovation-and-maintenance/renovation/application-for-a-renovation-permit)

## Voice and evidence rules

- Write directly to the reader. Define technical terms in the text and glossary. Avoid implying that confidence, a video, or owning a tool establishes competence.
- State assumptions for every time or cost range: place, date, project size, material grade, and whether tools, disposal, permits, tax, and labor are included.
- Attribute safety, code, permit, licensing, insurance, warranty, and financial claims. Prefer current government/regulator, standards body, manufacturer, insurer/policy, and trade-authority documents relevant to the target market.
- Distinguish a legal requirement from a recommendation and from a common practice. Avoid universal phrases such as “always,” “never,” and “insurance will deny” unless a source supports the exact scope.
- Use concise numbered source notes in the chapter and maintain the complete reference list in Appendix G. The S-numbers in the claim log are internal tracking IDs, not final citation numbers. Log the retrieval date and jurisdiction for changeable sources.
- Label fictional, composite, or illustrative case studies. Do not present estimates or accidents as observed data without a source.

## Outline conflicts to settle in the draft

- Section 6 allows some permitted yellow work; section 7 lists anything requiring a permit or inspection as red. Use the conditional rule above and explain that local law can prohibit DIY work regardless of color.
- Tub recaulk appears as green while shower waterproofing is red. Distinguish a visible, replaceable seal from the concealed waterproofing system; escalate if water damage or failed waterproofing is suspected.
- Mounting and gutter cleaning appear green but can involve concealed utilities, heavy loads, ladders, and falls. Assign the routine scope only, with explicit limits and stop conditions.
- “Insurance denied claims” and similar statements need policy-specific support. Explain possible coverage and liability questions without asserting automatic denial.
- The heading calls this a whitepaper, while the user wants a book. Use book structure and a source-backed, practical style once the author confirms the format.
- Section 6.2 presents DIY electrical replacements. For Singapore readers this conflicts with EMA's licensed-worker guidance; rewrite it as a hiring and inspection profile, and adjust the skill roadmap and case study to match.
