# Appendix F: Plain-language glossary

These terms describe the book's Singapore HDB setting. They are aids to reading, not substitutes for current authority guidance or a qualified person's assessment.

| Term | Meaning in this book |
| --- | --- |
| HDB flat | A flat governed by Housing & Development Board rules relevant to renovation and maintenance. |
| Green light | A small, reversible task a prepared beginner can usually attempt after checking the particular flat and product. |
| Yellow light | A task whose conditions or consequences require additional checks, skill, and a firm stopping point; a professional may be the right choice. |
| Red light | A task this book directs the homeowner to brief and commission from an appropriately qualified professional, rather than execute as DIY. |
| Permit | Formal approval for a specified scope of work. Whether a permit is needed is a separate question from who may legally carry out the work. |
| HDB condition | A rule or restriction attached to work in an HDB flat, including work for which no renovation permit is required. |
| Directory of Renovation Contractors (DRC) | HDB's directory for contractors eligible to undertake HDB renovation work. Directory listing is not a guarantee of workmanship. |
| Licensed Electrical Worker (LEW) | A person licensed by the Energy Market Authority to perform electrical work within the scope of the licence. |
| Licensed Plumber (LP) | A person licensed by PUB for regulated plumbing work. PUB also lists certain simple plumbing works; check the current rules and exact task. |
| Licensed Gas Service Worker | A worker licensed for gas service work within the licence scope. Piped-gas work is a professional boundary in this book. |
| Qualified Person (QP) | A registered architect or professional engineer acting in the capacity required for certain building proposals. The needed discipline and approvals depend on the work. |
| Approved Window Contractor (AWC) | A contractor approved for specified HDB window installation or repair work. |
| Scope | The exact surfaces, fixtures, rooms, and operations included in a job. A change of scope can change permits, skills, and risk. |
| Substrate | The underlying surface to which paint, sealant, tile, an anchor, or another finish is applied. Its condition determines whether the finish will hold. |
| Screed | A levelling layer beneath a floor finish; changing it may affect floor height and waterproofing conditions. |
| Waterproofing membrane | A continuous system intended to keep water from entering the structure or neighbouring spaces. A bead of cosmetic sealant is not a replacement for it. |
| Grout | Material filling the joints between tiles. Cracked grout may be visible evidence of movement or moisture, but the cause must be found before repair. |
| Floor trap | The drain fitting in a wet area. Debris at its accessible opening differs from a deeper blockage in the drainage system. |
| Shutoff | A valve or control used to stop a service, such as water. Identify the correct control before starting work that could release water. |
| Consumer unit | The flat's electrical distribution board, containing protective devices. Its internal work belongs with a Licensed Electrical Worker. |
| Residual current device (RCD/RCCB) | A protective device intended to disconnect electricity when it detects certain leakage currents. It does not make live electrical work safe. |
| Concealed service | A cable, pipe, or other service hidden in a wall, floor, or ceiling. Its unknown position is a reason to stop drilling or cutting. |
| Anchor or fixing | The fastener system holding an item to a wall or ceiling. Suitability depends on the actual wall, load, position, and product instructions. |
| Load | The weight or force a fitting or structure must carry, including use after installation. |
| Cure time | The period a product needs to reach its specified performance. Follow the particular manufacturer's instructions before using the area. |
| Rework | Time and material spent correcting an unsatisfactory result. Include it when comparing DIY with a quote. |
| Contingency | Money and time reserved for a defined uncertainty, such as discovering a damaged substrate. It does not make an unsafe task acceptable. |
| Quote | A contractor's priced offer for a stated scope, exclusions, materials, and timeline. Compare like scopes rather than headline totals. |
| Handover record | The documents and evidence received after work, such as approvals, test results where applicable, product details, and warranty terms. |

For current permit and trade definitions, use the authority links in [Appendix G](G-references-and-standards.md).
