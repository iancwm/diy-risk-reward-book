# Appendix H — Sample project plans

These are **illustrative HDB scenarios**, not accounts of completed work. Amounts are dated product inputs or blank quote fields; times are planning allowances. Each plan starts with the precise scope because a changed scope may change the colour, approval, and worker requirements. Use the [risk form](B-risk-assessment-form.md) and [permit checklist](C-permit-trigger-checklist.md) for a real flat.

## Plan 1 — Green: replace four cabinet handles

**Outcome:** Four existing cabinet doors have matching handles that operate smoothly, with the old holes reused and no new drilling.

| Planning field | Illustrative entry |
| --- | --- |
| Flat and location | Existing HDB kitchen, dry cabinet doors |
| Scope | Remove four old handles; fit four handles with the same hole centres using suitable supplied screws |
| Excluded | New holes, damaged door repair, modification near wiring or built-in appliances |
| Colour and reason | Green if the hole centres and screw lengths match; small, visible, reversible finish work |
| Product input | Two IKEA NYDALA two-packs at S$9.90 each = **S$19.80**, listed 28 September 2026[^handles] |
| Other cost fields | Delivery S$___; replacement screws S$___; total S$___ |
| Time allowance | About 1 hour for measurement, fitting, and cleanup; adjust for the actual doors |
| Tools | Ruler, suitable hand screwdriver, soft cloth, container for old screws |
| Before starting | Measure hole centres and door thickness; confirm screws will not protrude through the front; protect finish |
| Stop if | Hole centres differ, doors are swollen or split, or the supplied screws cannot clamp the handle securely |
| Acceptance | All handles align, doors close freely, screws stay secure after normal use |
| Record | Product and screw details, receipt, before/after photographs |

**Decision note:** A new hole pattern is a different scope. Reassess visible finish damage, concealed services in adjacent panels, and whether a cabinet specialist would be more economical.

## Plan 2 — Yellow: replace a kitchen mixer tap

**Outcome:** A worn tap is replaced without leaks, damage to the countertop or supply connections, or unresolved compliance questions. This plan deliberately stops at homeowner planning and inspection; PUB's current simple-work list and HDB conditions must be checked for the exact replacement.[^pub][^hdb-plumbing]

| Planning field | Illustrative entry |
| --- | --- |
| Flat and location | HDB kitchen, existing sink and water points |
| Scope | Like-for-like tap replacement only, subject to current rules and a sound existing installation |
| Excluded | Pipe rerouting, hidden pipe repair, gas, waterproofing, electrical work, or altering the sink support |
| Colour and reason | Yellow because water release and hidden connection condition can change the consequence quickly |
| Product input | IKEA LAGAN mixer tap listed at **S$59** on 28 September 2026[^tap] |
| Quote fields | Tap S$59; compatible fittings S$___; licensed plumber if needed S$___; contingency S$___; total S$___ |
| Time allowance | Set a half day aside for checks, installation or professional visit, leak observation, and cleanup; obtain a job-specific estimate |
| Before starting | Confirm model dimensions and connection compatibility; locate and test the correct water shutoff without disturbing the tap; check current worker rules |
| Stop if | Shutoff fails, a valve or pipe is corroded or moves, connection type differs, water persists after shutoff, or a leak appears after work |
| Acceptance | Dry connections under observation, normal hot/cold operation where applicable, stable tap, no cabinet or floor moisture |
| Record | Product instructions, receipt, worker details if hired, photographs, and any warranty |

**Hiring decision:** If any stop sign appears, leave the water isolated if safe and arrange a Licensed Plumber or other appropriate service. A cheap tap does not make a damaged supply line a beginner project.

## Plan 3 — Red: investigate a bathroom ceiling leak

**Outcome:** The source is identified and the responsible parties agree on an appropriate repair and follow-up. A ceiling stain is evidence of moisture, not proof that repainting solves it. HDB describes different routes for leaks between flats and leaks related to common property.[^ceiling]

| Planning field | Illustrative entry |
| --- | --- |
| Flat and location | HDB bathroom with a recurring ceiling stain |
| Scope | Document symptoms, notify the relevant owner/neighbour or Town Council as appropriate, arrange assessment and qualified repair |
| Excluded | Homeowner hacking, membrane replacement, pipe alteration, ceiling demolition, or painting over an active leak |
| Colour and reason | Red for diagnosis and repair of concealed water intrusion and possible shared-flat responsibility |
| Cost plan | Assessment S$___; repair quote S$___; access/restoration S$___; contingency S$___; responsibility and payment arrangement to document |
| Time plan | Photograph and report promptly; obtain an inspection date and written repair schedule from the responsible parties |
| Immediate safety check | Keep water away from electrical fittings; if a fitting is wet or there is an electrical concern, stop using it and seek qualified help |
| Questions for the professional | What is the confirmed source? Which flat or common element is affected? What tests support the finding? What approvals and worker qualifications apply? What work is included and excluded? |
| Acceptance | Source and remedy documented; no renewed moisture during the agreed observation period; affected finishes restored after drying |
| Record | Dated photographs, messages, inspection report, HDB/Town Council correspondence where relevant, quote, approvals, invoices, warranty |

**Decision note:** The homeowner's role is to preserve evidence, coordinate access, ask for a diagnosis, and check the repair result. The repair scope must come from the investigation.
